GUARDIAN

Privacy policy

Last updated: 15 October 2026. Applies to Guardian for Android and iPhone.

Guardian helps you step away from pornography. Because of what it is for, it is built so that nothing about you or what you do leaves your phone.

What Guardian collects

Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. Guardian has no account, no sign-up, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party tracking code. We, the developers, never receive any information about you from the app.

What Guardian keeps on your phone

To work, Guardian stores the following on your device only:

  • your progress: clean days, streaks, journey stage, jewels and items
  • things you write or choose in the app: your reasons ("your why"), journal entries, urge logs, missions, message preferences, and the personal photo if you add one
  • on Android, the web addresses (domains) that raised the curtain, and any sites you add to the blocklist yourself

On Android this data is excluded from Google cloud backups and from phone-to-phone transfer. On iPhone, Guardian's saved progress and journal are excluded from iCloud and computer backups. Uninstalling Guardian deletes it from your phone.

Android: browser monitor (Accessibility service)

Guardian uses Android's Accessibility service to read the address bar of your browser, so it can recognise an adult site and show the curtain at that moment. The address is checked on your phone against a list stored on your phone. It is not recorded beyond the domains listed above, and it is never transmitted. Guardian does not read passwords, messages or other content of the pages you visit.

When you choose "I've got this" on the curtain, Guardian also uses this service to replace that page in your browser tab with a blank page, so it is not waiting there the next time you open the browser. It does nothing else in your browser. You turn this on yourself in Android settings, and can turn it off at any time.

Android: DNS filter (optional VPN)

If you turn on the optional DNS filter, Guardian uses Android's VPN feature to see the names of sites your apps look up, so it can recognise adult sites outside the browser. Only name lookups pass through it, never the content of your traffic. Lookups are answered by your own network's resolvers, and Guardian does not log or send them anywhere. It is off unless you turn it on.

iPhone: Safari extension

Guardian's Safari extension checks the address of each page you open in Safari against a list built into the extension, on your phone, and shows the curtain on adult sites. Safari asks you to allow it on all websites because it has to check every address; it does not read the content of the pages you visit. If you choose "Continue anyway", the extension remembers that site for 15 minutes in Safari's storage on your phone so the curtain doesn't return during that time. Nothing is transmitted.

iPhone: Screen Time (optional)

If you turn on Extra protection, Guardian uses Apple's Screen Time framework. Strict mode turns on Apple's own adult-content filter, and any apps or sites you choose to shield are handled by iOS. Apple gives Guardian private tokens for your choices, not the names of the apps or sites, and Guardian keeps them on your phone.

Notifications

Guardian's reminders are scheduled on your phone. No notification server is involved.

Network access

On Android, Guardian's only network request downloads the public list of adult sites it uses for recognition, from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com). That request sends nothing about you; as with any download, GitHub can see the IP address it came from. The iPhone app makes no network requests.

If you write to us

If you send feedback or a question by email, we receive your email address and what you write, and use them only to reply to you. You can ask us to delete your messages at any time.

Age

Guardian is intended for adults aged 18 and over.
